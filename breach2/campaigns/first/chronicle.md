# The chronicle of N Division, Nine Bridges

## Day 1

The ledger read at dawn: one breach, #4, a grade 1 in Vinegar Yard, goblins in the cooperage
loft of Harker's Brewery. Given to the Owl: Probationer Albert Pike and Ives, a month on the
seat and nothing yet to show for it. Elks and jellies still loose in the Yard, fire in the
Gasworks cemetery.

The Owl walked to Malt Lane. The loft door opens on a wood at night; the owl went in ahead
and found three saddled warhorses grazing in a glade, and a path on into fog.
They went round the horses. Pike trod on a branch near the far side and the horses came about
and stamped, but let them go. In the fog beyond, a small copper dragon stepped onto the path
and spoke to them in no language either of them had.
Ives drew the gizmo in the air for it, and the dragon understood that well enough: two of
them, wanting home, walked the pair through the fog to a thorn hedge. Beyond it the owl
counted eight goblins camped at the anchor.
Ives offered the goblins the way home and was not believed. Their captain, Nettle, set a
price instead: their horses, the dragons kept off, and twenty gold for the trouble.
Pike chose to fight. It lasted two rounds. Ives put the captain to sleep on her feet; a
sentry's arrow and a scimitar took Pike to 3 HP at the gap, and he cut the goblin with the
scimitar in half. Then the two dragons came over the hedge and breathed acid down both lines,
and five goblins died where they stood. Two are left, one dazed and one on his knees. Pike
absorbed the lot: 300 XP, level 2, and Ives with him.
Pike would not kill the two that were left, nor the horses. The goblins fetched their own
mounts through the fog; Ives folded out the gizmo, and the captain, the boy, three horses and
two dragons stood by it and went home with the wood. Breach #4 closed before noon on day 1: six attended to, seven
resolved. It left a copper scale on the loft floor, and 110 GP.
Ives took up the scale. At the station the Director took the report as written, Mr Vale wrote
"seven not attended to" in his own book, and Dr Lark owned that the grade had been wrong.
The afternoon went on steel: a shield and a battleaxe for Pike, studded leather for Ives,
fitted in the cellar shop by Meg Stubbs, who had him hum for her while she worked.

## Days 2 to 40

Forty days without a grade 1. The Owl reported every dawn and was sent nowhere.

The Wren had the Yard's breaches: a white dragon on day 2 that left frost on the brewery
roofs for a week, a shark in a cistern, a green dragon, a devil. The Raven's Berserkers, on
day 7, walked out into Vinegar Yard before she reached them; she closed the breach on day
10 and the men who had left it are the Yard's for good. The jellies went into the brewery
drains and the brewers scalded them out with boiling wort. The Stag rode down the elks on a
rest day, and the Yard ate elk.

On day 16 the Lion came out of the Grick breach in Lime Wharf and would not go back. It has
been open since with nobody on it, and has spilled twice. On day 22 the Bear put out the
Gasworks cemetery with a fire-brigade pump. A Giant Ape breach in Chapel Fields waited two
days for the Fox and spilled; a Pit Fiend in Tallow Row spilled the day after it opened,
and the Fox closed the breach on the 38th with whatever came out of it still in the
streets. The music hall shut.

Mr Vale went up to Head Office for the quarter's audit on day 3 and came back on day 38,
his hour with Pike still in his book. On day 19 a crate of blank cores came down to the
cellar for Meg Stubbs. Ives carried it, and said on the stair, to nobody, that it was warm.

## Day 41

Twenty days to review, and Pike still 150 XP short of the level the Board expects of him.
The ledger at dawn: the Bear closed the Aboleth, the Grick breach spilled again, and **#19**,
a grade 1, Giant Wolf Spider, opened in Vinegar Yard: the Tun Street public baths. Given to
the Owl.

Ives knocked on the baths' street door, and a castaway opened it: Tom Rudd, with Will Pegg
behind him on a beach where the front hall should be, two seamen of the *Kestrel* marooned
three weeks before for stealing. They had seen the city from that door at dawn and wanted
none of their beach back. They told the Owl what was further in: two of their old shipmates
filling casks up the creek, and something in the roots past the big tree.

Pike told them plainly that the beach would go home and they would have to go with it, and
walked on. Rudd said "Aye", in the voice of a man who has been told things by officers
before. Over the dunes, at the creek's mouth, the Owl found the *Kestrel*'s jolly boat and the
two who had rowed it in.

Pike told them where they were. Bess Harrow didn't believe it until Ives rowed her down the
creek into the fog and the jolly boat's bow knocked against white tiles and a brass rail
lettered NO DIVING. She sat a while with her hand on the tile. Then she asked how to get home.

Pike told Bess the two thieves were alive; she spat in the creek and stayed by her boat. At
the big tree he sent the owl into the hollow. It saw nothing, and something took it off a
root in one bite.

Pike chose to attend to the spiders. Ives oiled the root arch and lit it, and Pike stood in
the gap. Two bites in the first six seconds took twenty of his twenty-five hit points. He
killed two and the fire killed one; he went down, Ives sang him up, he killed a fourth and
went down again, and a spider came over the arch and took Ives to pieces in the water. The
two sailors from the creek came at Ives's shout and killed the last four with their knives.
They were twenty seconds too late. **Probationer Albert Pike, the Owl, died at the anchor of
breach #19 on day 41**, forty-two days on the seat, 500 XP, one breach closed and nobody
killed in it that he could spare. Ives's body was wrecked beside him.

The two castaways on the beach broke the street door that same hour and are loose in the
Yard. Mrs Bright ran to the station. The Director sent the Raven in to fetch, not to close:
she and Sedge brought out Pike, and Ives's core in Sedge's hand. The sailors at the creek
were told to wait for the next Owl.

## Days 42 to 47

The seat stood empty six days. Meg Stubbs could not wake Ives's core: it had gone dark
when Pike did, and no bench can bind a core twice. She kept it three days, then crated it for
Head Office. The copper scale came off his ear as plain copper. The Lion hunted
down the loose Gricks in Lime Wharf himself, and still would not go back into their breach.
The Hound closed a Shield Guardian in Lime Wharf; the Wren closed a Grick in the Yard. The
Tun Street baths stayed a beach, with two sailors in it eating Mrs Bright's bread.

On day 47 the transfer came: **Probationer Bob Fletcher**, an orc, a soldier, a ranger with a
longbow, who asked the ledger clerk on his first morning how many days there were to review.
Fourteen. With him came a new automaton, **Rookwood**, a druid, a day old and a stranger to
the seat. Breach #19, the Tun Street baths, still open, was read out and given to the Owl again.

Morning, the station. The Owl came up from the cellar stair with the gizmo case on the flags
and the call box on Rookwood's strap, and stood a while at the wall. Rookwood had read the
Raven's account twice and offered the economical course: walk into the baths, talk the creek
round, set the apparatus under the tree, and go home with nobody struck. The Director came
down the stair with his ledger under his arm. Ledger short, Static quiet, in that order;
review in fourteen days, and he would rather not write the Owl up as behind.

Midday, Tun Street. The Owl went in by the street door, under the sacking, with the brass man
and the case. The front hall was empty but for the tide mark and two sets of boots going out.
The creek had two sailors in it who had been waiting six days and stood up when they saw the
call box. They walked the Owl to the boiler room, and the brass man stopped at the root arch
and said he had never been in the room and did not need the Raven to tell him what it was.
The case is up under the roots. The run has not begun.

Late morning. The run took ten minutes in the boiler room. The brass man hummed the mast's note
the whole time, very low, and did not seem to know he was doing it. Bess and Ned stood still on
their roots and counted. At the ninth minute Bess put Pike's battleaxe into the Owl's hands,
haft first, and said it was for whoever came with a brass man. The piece went home at the
tenth, and the two sailors went with it, Ned's last words a message for Will Pegg about a boat.
The float and the belt went too, because nobody had picked them up. The Owl came out with a
cloak of grey-green hide, lying on the root where the belt had been. The castaways in the Yard
are here for good now. The baths are a cellar again, with a tide mark on the brick.

Midday. The Owl sat an hour on the root with the cloak across his shoulders and came up
attuned, with the damp gone out of it. On the step Mrs Bright said the two from the beach had
come by for bread and asked where a man might sleep dry, and she had sent them up Brewer's Lane
to the malt loft. Asked after the Berserkers, the brass man said they were loose in the Yard
since day eight, from the Raven's breach, and that the ledger reads them out each dawn. Nobody
has seen them. Mrs Bright would like it to stay that way. The Owl went up the lane towards
the brewery.

Afternoon-ish, the malt loft. The Owl put the first arrow into the broad one with the cutlass, and the brass man laid the plants out under the two of them, as he had proposed. The plants held nobody. The broad one threw daggers from too far off and missed twice, and the thin one put two daggers into the brass man, who went down on the cobbles with the second one, and the plants went back into the boards with his concentration. The broad one is at nine hitpoints and marked. The thin one is whole and at the hoist door. The Owl is still on his feet.

The broad one fell to the Owl's second arrow, a critical, with the brass man still on the cobbles beside him. The thin one looked at his friend, and at the Owl, and put the dagger down on the sacks. He asked that Tom Rudd be buried where the drain could not have him, and that the Owl leave him alone. He will work the brewery drays for the Saturday pay. The brass man is breathing, if not much, and will be kitted by the Owl next. The purser's money is still under the sacks, and nobody has said a word about it.

Round four, the yard. The Owl knelt by the brass man and used the kit on him, and the brass man stopped going down. He will wake in an hour with a point of his life back, and no more than that. Pegg came to the hoist door, looked down at them both, and went back to Tom Rudd by the sacks. Nobody has said yet whether Tom is going into the ground or into the brewery's dung cart.

The Owl took Rudd's body to the yard's corner with Pegg and two brewery men holding the lantern, and Pegg put him in the ground by the mash-house wall without a word, with Mrs Bright's loaf on the stone. Rookwood came round an hour later, asked very politely whether the Probationer had been hit, and remembered he had. The station took the report in the morning, and Crane said Lime Wharf had a grade five standing open with nobody to take it, and that he would not ask the Owl. Yet. At dawn Finch read out the day's news: a grade nine at Needle Street that the Fox will take; the Pit Fiends of Tallow Row gone off the list, and the cellar of the music hall bolted from the inside; the Berserkers still loose in the Yard; and Lime Wharf still standing open. Stubbs watched the brass man over her tea, and said, to nobody, that he was warm.

Day 48, midday. The Owl went out to hunt the Berserkers the Raven had left in the Yard. Fletcher
read the trail through the soot on the first try, and it led to the old coal arch under the
viaduct at the west end of the Yard, where three men have been living on brewery sacks since
day 7. They had not seen the pair come. The arch is quiet but for a fire and a whetstone.

Midday. The brass man and the Owl came up on the coal arch with the three men inside, and the
Owl opened with his bow before they were awake. The plants took one of them, and the Owl put two
arrows and a flame into him, and he tore free in round two. The other two came through the
plants with their axes. Round three: two greataxes met the brass man in the mouth of the arch,
and he went down among them, unconscious, with a point of his life left. The Owl is standing
in the Yard with the three of them in front of him.

The brass man took a hit from the first man in the mouth of the arch that wrecked him, and the
Owl brought him up again with a prayer and a handful of spells, and the second man took him down
again. The third came up through the mouth of the arch with his axe and finished it. The Owl ran.
He ran west through the Yard, past the brewery and the cooper's, and nothing followed him past
the sacks. Behind him, the three men stood over what was left of Rookwood, at the mouth of the
arch, and the brass man's core is still in the wreck with his case.

Midday. The Owl turned back east across the Yard, and walked until he was out of reach of the
three men at the arch mouth, nine squares short of it. He stopped there, with the rush spent on
the way out and the bow across his back, and looked at the wreck the length of the Yard away.
Nobody came out after him.

Midday. The Owl went back in on the Rush and took the brass man's core out of the wreck, under the
men's noses, and fell back. They came after him as far as the street and caught him. The first
took two points of his life off and the second took the rest, and the Owl dropped to one point,
and the core is in his pack. The men stand over him now, with their axes, and the arch behind
them has the brass man's case in it.

Day 48, the coal arch street. The Owl took the core and was caught on the way out. Rookwood, the
automaton, had gone dark at the arch mouth the round before, and his core went home with the
Owl in his pack; the case stayed where it fell. Bob Fletcher went down on the cobbles of the Yard

What Rookwood knew of it he knew from the inside of a wreck: a brass man who had been a day old

## Days 49 to 57

The Owl's seat stood empty for eight days, which is what the Board's dice came to. The station ran as it runs.

Day 49, the dawn after the coal arch: the wall took Bob Fletcher's name under Albert Pike's. The Raven had fetched the body the dusk before. Day 50 was quiet. On day 51 a Dragon Turtle opened in Tallow Row, and the Giant Apes went out of Chapel Fields down the canal to the Ropewalk slaughterhouse; the bargemen were paid and nobody asked what for. The Bear closed the Turtle on day 52. On day 53 the Grick breach at Lime Wharf (#8) spilled out onto the wharf and was struck off the ledger; its Gricks are the wharf's problem now.

Day 55: a Knight opened at Lime Wharf, grade four, the Reader's figure, with nobody to take it. On day 56 the Wolf, Thomas Pendry, Sergeant, went into it by the street door with Jarvis, his brass man. They did not come out. The Raven went to the door at dusk on the Director's order and came back with nothing but what she had seen. A Chimera opened at Clock Street the same day, grade five, and the Lion's.

Day 57, dawn: the Board's transfer office sent up a Probationer, Tom Thorne, an orc out of the ranks, with a Sorcerer's automaton called Hobbes. Hobbes had the Board's spare case on his back and had never seen a breach. He knows nothing of the seat.

**Day 57, the station, mid-morning.** The Mechanic has the cloak on the bench in the cellar shop, laid out under a lamp the way one lays out a dead man's coat. It is grey oilskin, soft and cold, and the room has the mast's note in it. "Uncommon," the Mechanic says, "and made for the last one. It's been waiting." Hobbes stands by the fabricator with the case at his feet and does not touch the cloak.

Upstairs the Director does not get up. He says there is nothing at the Owl's grade this morning. There is a grade four at Lime Wharf that the Wolf went into and did not come out of, and a grade five at Clock Street that is the Lion's. The Raven's report is that there are armoured men in the first room of the Wharf breach, standing on the floor where the Wolf went down, and that they have not come out. "I can send you," the Director says. "I'd rather you didn't go. The Board has never once asked me what I'd rather."

Hobbes, at the door: "I'm sorry for the Wolf's brass man, sir. I mention it only for completeness."
