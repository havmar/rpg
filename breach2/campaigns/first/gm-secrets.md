# GM secrets · N Division, Nine Bridges (seed 426)

Not for the human. True levels, unexplored rooms, unheard news, the answer.

## Why the breaches open

**The city is itself a breach, and the masts are what keep it open.** It has no edge because
it is a pocket stapled onto nowhere; left alone it would "be itself again in a day", which
is to say gone. A mast is not a ward that holds the world shut. It is a gizmo run the
wrong way round, and the ears do not listen: they **pull**. Pieces of other realities are dragged in
near a mast in proportion to how hard it strains, weakly under it (where it is anchored
well), violently in the corners where two masts' pulls fight. That is why grade follows reach.

The masts burn the magic denizens leak. It can't be bottled; it can only be absorbed by a
person. So the Board grows officers on rationed breaches, and at level 20 the Commander is
"called to Head Office", where they are rendered into **cores**: big ones for masts, small
ones for automatons. Every automaton's core is a piece of a dead veteran. That is why they
are people, why they have that manner of old service, why a core can drive a gizmo, and why
Head Office's veterans do not write. Head Office believes, with reason, that the alternative
is ten million people ceasing to exist. Sending denizens home feeds nothing, which is why the
Board sets a quota of growth and the auditor frowns at "matters resolved".

Ives's core was cut from a Commander of another district; he never knew. It went dark when
Pike died (day 41) and has gone back to Head Office in the same kind of crate the blank ones
come in. **Rookwood's** core is another such piece: under stress at an anchor he sometimes
knows a room he has never seen. A core whose officer dies is not waste to Head Office.

**Clues, in rough order:** the Reader's misreads cluster on days the mast is under load; the
fitter will not say where blank cores come from, and they arrive warm; an automaton working the
gizmo hums the mast's note; denizens say they were *pulled*, not that they fell; the auditor's
ledgers price a breach in levels gained; a letter from a called Commander, never posted; a district
whose mast failed is not on any tram map.

## Station people's secrets

- **Crane** knows what Head Office does with Commanders and has decided not to think about it. He let Pike back because a seat empty twice in two months is an audit.
- **Dr Lark** has noticed the load pattern and has stopped writing it down.
- **Finch** (the Clerk) reads every officer's report before the Director does, and keeps copies. (There are no calls any more: the call box left the game on day 57.)
- **Stubbs** suspects what cores are; the automatons have, in their sleepless hours, half told her.
- **Vale** is honest and is the only one who would be horrified. He thinks Pike's walk-out hides a misappropriation.
- **Pike** walked out on day -20 after watching the Raven's pair kill a room of things that were begging. Came back on day -17 because there was nowhere else.
- **Mary Dunn**, the Owl before Pike, died on day -31 in a grade-1 that was truly a grade 2.
- **Stubbs** tried for three days to wake Ives's core and could not; she noticed the crate it left in.
- **Bob Fletcher** asked for the transfer to get off a Static corner in another district; he means to do his sixty days and put in for the clerks.

- **Rules from day 57:** no denizen settles. Any still living in the city from earlier days (Will Pegg, labouring in Vinegar Yard) is owed a sending or a death, and the mast hears him; put him on the Owl's desk when it suits. Station people are spoken of by title in play: Crane the Director, Lark the Reader, Finch the Clerk, Stubbs the Mechanic, Vale the Auditor.

## Closed: #19, the Tun Street baths (day 47)

**#19 · grade 1 · truly level 2 · seed 727593** (`gen_breach.py --seed 727593 --level 2 --reported 1`).

river's mouth, the wolf spiders' country. 1,200 XP inside; Pike needs 150 for level 3.

| Room | Denizens | XP | Disposition | Size | Feature | Coins |

- **Room 1, the beach** (the front hall): coral rubble and a stove-in longboat. **Tom Rudd and
  Will Pegg**, marooned three weeks ago by their own ship for stealing from the purser, with
  a cask of water and their daggers. They don't want home: home is a beach where they were
  left to starve. They have looked out of the street door at dawn and seen a city with
  bread in it. Convincing them to go back would take something real; leaving them is two
  pirates loose in the Yard (and two men who mostly want a meal and a berth).
- **Room 2, the creek** (the plunge hall): knee-deep brown water between roots. **Bess
  Harrow and Ned Coyle**, from the same ship, ashore this morning to fill casks; the ship
  stood off the bar. They have not been out of the creek and think the fog that came down
  at dawn is weather; they will not believe the ship is gone without proof. Once they
  believe it, they want to go home (the ship is there, waiting for them, a few hours). They
  rowed Rudd and Pegg ashore three weeks ago and left them; the two pairs will fight on
  sight unless someone stops it.
- **Room 3, the anchor** (the boiler room): a hollow under a great mangrove, roots for a
  roof, the floor humming. A brood of **eight giant wolf spiders** on the roots overhead
  (Stealth +7). Beasts: not argued with. They hunt what moves on the ground and leave what
  is still; fire and smoke drive them; meat draws them. Ives's *Animal Friendship* works on
  them (INT 3), one per casting.
- **Relic** on closing: the GM's pick, Uncommon or below, of this coast.

Marks (day 41, Pike): room 1's castaways were told they must go home, and broke out through
the street door within the hour: **loose in Vinegar Yard**, with the purser's 40 GP (the

**Day 47, the walk in.** Rookwood had told the Owl the castaways are out in the Yard and stay
