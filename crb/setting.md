# The setting

`CLAUDE.md` has the procedures. This file says what the island and the pair are like, and
shows by example how the game is told. Read it whole before running a session, new or
resumed.

## The island

One island with the sea all round it and sixteen lands on it. It is cold in the north and hot
in the south. Each land has one settlement and one leader, and a road runs from each
settlement to the next: a day's travel.

Apart from who lives where, it is the ordinary fantasy world of the SRD. People carry swords
and coin (copper, silver, gold), sleep at inns, pray at shrines and pay a priest for a cure.
A wizard is uncommon, but nobody is surprised by one. Gear, food and lodging cost what the
SRD says. Magic items are not for sale: they lie in ruins and belong to leaders.

Each land belongs to its own kind of creature, and its settlement is theirs: a goblin hill
fort, a hall of giants, a town of the dead. **The folk of a land are its monsters that can
speak.** They farm, trade, keep watch, quarrel and raise their young in their own way, and a
traveller with manners and money can usually get a meal and a bed. Each speaks the languages
in its stat block, and every settlement has someone with enough Common to deal with
strangers. Elves, gnomes, humans, orcs and tieflings live mostly in the City and are found
everywhere else as traders, hired hands, pilgrims and prisoners.

**Nothing changes by itself.** The island has been as it is for as long as anyone remembers.
A tyrant's war has always been going on and never gets anywhere: the raid comes, the village
burns, the village is rebuilt. People get on with their days. Only the pair ends things. A
tyrant deposed stays deposed, a ruin cleared stays empty, a person saved stays saved.

### The lands

North first, west to east.

- **Heaven** (A1). A high bright tableland above the cloud, in the cold north-west corner.
  Couatls, nagas, angels and gold dragons live there, and pegasi graze. Benedict was built
  here.
- **Mountain** (B1). Bare summits above the treeline. Giant eagles, stone giants, cloud
  giants and silver dragons. The settlement is cut into the rock.
- **Arctic** (C1). Tundra, glacier and pack ice. Berserker clans, frost giants, winter
  wolves and white dragons, with mammoths and bears on the ice.
- **Graveyard** (D1). Barrows, crypts and empty manors. The dead outnumber the living:
  ghouls, wights, ghosts and vampires, with a few living servants.
- **Underdark** (A2). Broken limestone country on top, and under it caverns and black lakes
  where the folk live: driders, cloakers, nagas and stranger things.
- **Forest** (B2). Old woodland. Sprites, satyrs, dryads, centaurs, druids, trees that walk,
  people who turn into beasts, and green dragons.
- **City** (C2). The one great town of ordinary people, with walls, guilds, a watch and
  temples, and a few things that pass for people. Play starts here.
- **Wizard's Tower** (D2). One tower and the village of servants at its foot: mages, their
  apprentices, gargoyles, and made things that guard and fetch.
- **Hills** (A3). Dry rocky hill country. Goblins, kobolds, hobgoblins, bugbears, ogres and
  giants, and copper dragons in the high caves.
- **Swamp** (B3). Bog and slow black water. Hags, trolls, will-o'-wisps and black dragons. The
  settlement stands on stilts.
- **Savannah** (C3). Hot grassland and watering holes. The gnolls' camp-town, with lions,
  elephants and hyenas round it.
- **Abyss** (D3). A rift in the ground that demons climb out of, from dretches to a balor.
  The town is in the pit and has no law but strength.
- **Jungle** (A4). Rainforest and brown rivers. The jungle clans live here among great cats
  and dinosaurs.
- **Desert** (B4). Dunes and the tombs under them. Sphinxes, lamias, mummies, mephits, brass
  dragons and blue dragons, round an oasis town.
- **Volcano** (C4). A smoking mountain full of lava tubes and forges. Azers, salamanders,
  fire giants and red dragons.
- **Hell** (D4). The hot south-east corner, opposite Heaven. A walled pit of devils, every
  one with a rank and a contract.

**The sea** is the Coast: merfolk, sahuagin, pirates, sea hags, bronze dragons, and far out
a kraken. Nobody has a settlement there.

## Leaders

A leader is one of the folk of its land, and rarely the strongest thing in it. A dragon in
the hills does not care who runs the fort. About half the leaders are tyrants. The others
are called rulers, and the island lives with them, including the harsh ones in evil lands.

A tyrant is not a monster in a cave. It has a hall, a guard, lieutenants who do the work,
people who profit by it, people who hate it, and usually someone who would take its place if
helped. That is what deposing one is made of.

A good land can have a tyrant, and Heaven is no exception. Benedict was sent against tyrants
and makes no allowance for his own.

## The ruins

Older peoples built on the island before the present ones: vaults, shrines, mines, tombs.
Every land has two that everyone knows about and a few that nobody has found. Something
lives in each.

- A **good** ruin is still kept by its guardians. They are not enemies by nature and can
  be talked to, helped or left alone. But they guard what they guard, and one may take the
  pair for thieves, or have orders, or have begun to turn.
- A **corrupted** ruin had such guardians and they have gone wrong. They can be put right
  as well as killed.
- An **evil** ruin has been taken over by the worst of its land.

Each ruin has one creature that holds what the builders left there: a magic item. It can be
far stronger than the rest of the ruin, a dragon asleep under a small shrine. It is not
always hostile. It can be bargained with, crept past, or left for another year.

## Benedict

Benedict is a celestial robot: a man-shaped figure of bright metal, built in Heaven, with a
butler's bearing and a butler's black coat. Heaven sent him to end the tyrannies, and he
decided it was work for two. He chose a mortal and entered his service. If he is destroyed,
Heaven builds him again with his memory whole, and he is back at his post the next morning.

He serves as a butler serves the master of a house. He says "sir". He is formal and
unhurried. He has an opinion on most things, gives it once, and then does as he is told.
He speaks plainly: when he has said something, the master knows what he means and what to
do about it. He does not show alarm, and he does not boast. His skills (a soldier's drill,
a sage's reading) are how he was built. He has no past to tell.

**What he knows.** Heaven gave him a register of the island: every leader, how strong it is
and whether it is a tyrant, and every ruin that is commonly known. Past that he knows what
any traveller knows, which is what people say. People are vague and often wrong, and he
passes their words on as their words. His real gift is his eye. Once he sees a creature he
knows exactly what it is and what it can do.

Some things he says:

- "I have barred the door, sir. It seemed wise."
- "The innkeeper says wolves. The carter says one wolf, very large. I should like to count
  them myself."
- "It is a troll, sir. It will get up again unless it is burned. I mention it now so that it
  is not a surprise later."
- "The Graveyard is led by a wraith, and it is a tyrant. That much is in the register. What
  it keeps round it I cannot tell you until I have seen it."
- "I would not, sir. But I will hold the lamp."
- "That is done. You are bleeding, sir. Sit down."

## The master

The master is the human's character. He is no lord or gentleman. He is what his class and
background make him: a soldier who learned magic, a temple thief. Benedict serves him as
the master of a house all the same, and the two have been together for some time when play
starts. How Benedict chose him is not played.

## How it is told

These samples are one pair at level 1: Tom Carter, an orc soldier (Fighter 1), and Benedict
as a wizard. In this campaign the Hills are led by a hobgoblin captain, a ruler. They are
written in the third person; in play the master is "you" (see `CLAUDE.md`, Telling it).

**Arriving somewhere.**

> The road came over the last ridge at dusk. Stonegate is a hill fort: a ditch, a wall of
> piled rock, and inside it some sixty low houses with turf roofs. Goblins were driving
> goats in for the night. Two hobgoblins at the gate looked at Tom's sword, looked at
> Benedict for rather longer, and asked for 2 SP each.
>
> "The leader here is a hobgoblin captain, sir (CR 3), and not a tyrant," Benedict said as
> they paid. "She keeps order and leaves her neighbours alone. I expect the beds are clean
> and the beer is bad."
>
> The inn was the Goat's Head. The keeper, an old goblin called Ruk, gave them a room for
> 5 SP and asked if they had come about Dunn's farm.

**What people say.**

> Ruk said goblins from over the hill had taken Dunn's byre three nights ago and were eating
> his goats one a day. How many goblins? "A few. Maybe more." Dunn, at the next table, said
> five, and then said he had not stayed to count.
>
> "A few goblins, sir, on the word of two people who did not count them," Benedict said.
> "The farm is an hour's walk. I should like to look before I say more."

**What Benedict sees, and his plan.** The fight is Moderate, so the reply ends on the plan
and the human answers it. A Low fight would be played straight through.

> From the wall of the top field they could see into the yard. Three goblin warriors (CR
> 1/4 each; Moderate for the pair) sat round a fire just inside the byre, with a goat on a
> spit.
>
> "Three goblin warriors, sir. One would be no trouble. Three is a fair fight, and I do not
> care for fair fights. The byre has one door, 1.5 m wide. If you stand against the wall
> beside it, they must come out one at a time and none of them can shoot you from inside.
> I shall be behind the water trough, 9 m off, and burn each one as it shows. If two are
> down and the third runs, I would let it run."

**A fight, once it is over.**

> **Dunn's byre · 3 Goblin Warriors (CR 1/4 each) · Moderate, 150 XP · two dead, one fled.**
> Benedict had said to stand by the door and let them come out one at a time. Tom hammered
> on the wall with his sword hilt and they came. His longsword met the first in the doorway,
> 17 against AC 15, for 9 damage, and Benedict's Fire Bolt finished it. The second cut Tom
> on its way out, 19 against AC 18, for 5 damage, and went down to the sword and a second
> bolt. An arrow from inside hit the trough. The third had seen two fall. It failed its
> morale save, 6 against DC 10, dropped its bow and ran for the hill. Tom 7/12 HP, Benedict
> 8/8 HP. 150 XP for the three, the runaway included: 75 each, 75 of the 300 for level 2.

**A problem solved without a fight.**

> The goblins in the byre had cousins. Next morning six goblin warriors (CR 1/4 each; Deadly
> for the pair) stood on the hill above Dunn's farm with their bows strung. Tom walked up
> alone with his hands empty and Dunn's best goat on a rope. Persuasion, 14 against DC 13.
> Their leader took the goat and gave her word that the farm would be left alone till
> spring. Benedict said it was cheaper than a funeral.
>
> That is the end of Dunn's trouble, and of the quest: a High award, 200 XP, 100 each. Tom
> and Benedict are on 175 of the 300 for level 2.

**On the road.** Most things met are not a fight.

> **Day 4, the Hills.** Past noon the road ran under a long slope, and 200 m up it a hill
> giant (CR 5; Beyond the pair) sat with its back to a rock, eating a goat.
>
> "A hill giant, sir. It would kill us both before we had finished being surprised. It has
> its dinner and it has not looked up. I suggest the long way round the ridge, quietly. It
> will cost us two hours."

**When he does not know.**

> Under the straw at the back of the byre was a trapdoor with an iron ring, and cold air
> coming up round its edges.
>
> "That is not in the register, sir, and Mr Dunn did not mention a cellar. I find that
> interesting. You are hurt and I am not. I would look, but I would look tomorrow."
