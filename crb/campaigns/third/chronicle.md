# Chronicle

The story so far, in the GM's voice.

## Day 1: Hollin's mill

Northmen from the Arctic had taken Piet Hollin's mill on the Cold Brook, an hour north of the
City wall, and the Lord-Captain's Watch would not go out past the gate. Tom Yarrow and
Benedict paid the gate tax and went. Tom crept down the brook and counted four, three hungry
boys and their leader, Brann, who was more afraid of his chief, Grim Ulfrun, than of
anything in the south. Benedict walked into the yard with a sack of flour, and Tom talked.
Brann wanted silver to show in Ulfrun's hall, and Tom paid him ten gold rather than fight.
The northmen went home with the flour and the coin, and Hollin got his mill back without a
hole in it. He paid the ten gold back out of his own strongbox and promised them a bed for
as long as there was a mill.

## Day 1: Jory's song

Back in the City, carrying word to Hollin's daughter Wenna, they found her aunt Marta Ostler
with an empty cup on the shelf. Her son Jory, fifteen, had sung a song in the market about
where the wall tax goes, and under the Lord-Captain's law that meant the quarry. Before
curfew Tom went down to the Tanners' Row watch-house with his dice, lost five gold to
Corporal Hask with great good humour, and asked for the boy. Hask let him go. Benedict
scratched the cell lock with a pick so that it would look as if the boy had opened it
himself. Jory was home before the ninth bell, and Hask, who now had friends he didn't want,
told them over the last throw when the Lord-Captain leaves her Barbican.

## Days 2–3: the road west, and the Greenwell

On the road west a young green dragon watched them from a fallen oak and let them pass. In
Thornmoot, Grandfather Ashenroot told them the Greenwell's story: the builders shut
something old in the deep water with a black stone, and this spring the stone cracked, and
the black thread soured the sprites, the owls and his sister Mossmother. He gave Tom a leaf
from his crown and asked that none of them be killed. At the stone ring the sprites
remembered the leaf and lowered their bows. In the yew-hall the owls let them by. Tom and
Benedict came down the root-stair into the vault, both stronger for the day's work.

Mossmother stood over the spring with black in her eyes, and Tom talked to her about her
brother while Benedict walked to the stone with his hands empty. She let him. He knelt and
poured his holy water into the crack and prayed, and the crack closed, and the black thread
in the water thinned and was gone. The sprites' eyes cleared, and the owls', and last of all
Mossmother's. She gave them a vial of frost-glass the builders had left in her keeping. That
night in Thornmoot, Grandfather Ashenroot bowed to them, which nobody there had seen him do.

## Day 4: the moor road and Hvitmark

On the moor road into the Arctic a white dragon called Hrimgala came down out of the cloud
and asked for Benedict's gold sun. Tom gave her gold and a song, and the cold broke his lute
string on the second line, and she said it was a bad song and still wanted the sun. Benedict
levered it out of his breastplate with a crowbar and gave it to her, and she flew off with
it. They came into Hvitmark at dusk.

## Day 4, night: the boys on the ice

Brann's younger brother Eyvind and two other boys had come home empty-handed, and Ulfrun
had their furs taken and walked them out onto the ice. Tom and Benedict bought bearskins
and went after them in the dark. Two hours out they found the boys dug in under a pressure
ridge, with three ice mephits taking turns to breathe frost into the hole. Benedict walked
into the mouth of the hollow and cut the first in half with one stroke. Tom drove another
off with a whispered curse, and it came back in a fog with its friend and clawed him nearly
to the ground. Benedict laid hands on him and killed the second, and the third fled. They
brought the boys back across the ice wrapped in furs, and both of them were the stronger
for it.
They couldn't take the boys home: two of Ulfrun's sworn axes sat at a brazier across from
Brann's mother's door. So they took them to the Hearth by the gate, past the gate watch
without a sound, and asked old Ketil the keeper to hide them. His own son had walked out
onto the ice last winter and not come back. He put the boys in his peat store and wouldn't
take money for it, except one gold for the peat.

## Day 6: the wyrmling on the shore

Two of Ulfrun's axes came to the Hearth to tell them the road south was open, and Benedict
thanked them. Then Ketil sent them after the young white dragon that had been taking the
town's goats. They found its cave on the frozen shore with three ice mephits perched over
it, one of them the runaway from the ice. Tom's first arrow woke the dragon, and its breath
and the mephits' frost took Benedict half to pieces. Then a mephit's claw found the gap in
his armour and he went down on the shingle. Tom brought him up with a word, and Benedict
got to his feet and killed the dragon with his next stroke. The last of the mephits fled.
In the cave were sixteen gold pieces and a little bottle that smelled of moss.
