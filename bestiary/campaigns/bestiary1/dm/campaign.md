# Campaign prep · bestiary1

## The engine in one paragraph

A town that rents its valley from a dragon is short, by 1,200 to 2,300 GP this year
depending on the commons, and by about 1,500 every year after, because the mine that
made up the difference is dead. If the
rent fails, a devil takes about twenty people of three founding families, and the dragon
takes everything inside the walls. The people who aren't on the charter have paid the
head-tax for 80 years to keep the founders' necks safe, and this year they won't. A
devil's notary, quite open about what he is, is offering loans. The party: a thief who
left home to stop paying rent, and a wizard whose name is on the charter.

Revised after a second reading: the gap is **structural**, so loot can help but can't
answer it. The founders are landlords too (the commons pay them ground-rent); the
commons' strike is a rent strike. Everyone in this town is someone's tenant.

The question the campaign asks: **who pays, and with what, from now on?** Gold from the marches, the
commons' tax, souls through a loan, the founders' blood, or something nobody has thought
of yet (the charter rewritten, the dragon persuaded, the House outplayed). Nobody here is
a villain except, professionally, Sallow, and even he has a reason.

## The factions

### The Rent Office (the Landlady) · seed omen: Adult Red Dragon, faction

- **People:** the Landlady (never seen in town); **Ibbet Crane**, steward; kobolds at the
  Burnt Road toll-house.
- **Want:** the rent, on time, every year. She is a gold-lover and 4,000 GP a year
  forever beats one sack of a poor town. She is **not** indifferent; she is irritated:
  two years of late, grovelling, short-counted payments from the founders. She would
  rather have better tenants than burn the ones she has.
- **Ibbet** came to Lessing at 20 and has buried friends here. She can't extend the
  deadline, lend, or lie on the books. She takes rent in kind at resale value (half the
  SRD price), and only what the Landlady will have.
- **The amendment clause** is public: the charter can be changed by consent of both
  sides, witnessed, "upon payment of a thing the Lessor desires". No one has asked her in
  80 years what that would be. **What she desires is social, not an object**: to be
  dealt with as a party, not a weather. Lean towards: new Lessees who'd answer to her
  directly (the commons? a guild?), a seat (her steward's) on the council, or the founders
  coming to the Chimney in person to ask. Decide when someone actually asks; let the
  asking be what moves her.
- **Petitioning her:** the toll-house kobolds carry sealed letters north; she answers by
  her own letter, in Draconic, in about 5 days. To be received in person: a letter
  first, then the Burnt Road under her safe-conduct. Without it, the north road past the
  toll-house is hers and what lives there doesn't ask.
- **Means:** absolute. Not used unless the charter breaks.
- **Plan if nobody stops anything:** Day 30 at sundown, Ibbet counts the box. If it's
  short she writes DEFAULT in the ledger. At midnight the Erinyes arrives. At dawn the
  Landlady comes south.
- **Opinion of the party:** none yet. Ibbet likes people who pay on time and don't
  haggle.
- **Theft target:** the toll-house strongbox (about 200 GP): robbing the dragon to pay
  the dragon. If found out, it's a breach by the town? No: the charter binds the
  Lessees, not a vagrant thief. Ibbet would see it as theft from the Landlady, and say
  so to the Landlady.

### The Charterholders · the council

- **Aldous Ferrant**, mayor, 71: courteous, tired, ashamed. He has five great-
  grandchildren. He won't take the loan on others' souls, but he is wavering, and he
  hides that from Maud. Voice: long sentences that end in apologies.
- **Hesketh Ostry**, 50s, wool: practical to the point of cruelty, and honest about it.
  "The commons will lose one soul in ten households over thirty years. We lose all of
  our children in one night. Do the sums." He'll push the loan. Won't: lie about what it
  costs. Voice: numbers, shortness.
- **Clemency Dunmore**, 40s, the ferry: the swing vote. Plain, shrewd, distrusts Ostry,
  likes Wenna Tasker more than she'll admit. She'd vote against the loan if anyone showed
  her another way that kept the ground.
- **Aldous's secret:** last year he borrowed 900 GP from Sallow on the Ferrant house,
  1,000 to repay on Day 30. He'll lose the house either way. Maud doesn't know.
- **Ostry's secret:** 500 GP under his wool-store floor, to run with on Day 29. He
  presses the loan in public and plans to leave in private. If he's found out, the
  council is two votes, Ferrant and Dunmore.
- **Want:** the rent paid; their blood safe; their ground kept.
- **Means:** the council (law, the watch of six Guards, the Tally House), the ground
  (the land rolls in the Moot Hall), bounties. No money left in the open.
- **Why nobody fetched the ingots:** two crews of hired hands went last spring and
  neither came back. The founders have been afraid to send their own.
- **Bounty posted on Day 1:** the Old Tally strongroom's ingots (800 GP of silver),
  "recovered to the Moot Hall: 100 GP and one in ten of the recovered" (so 180 GP).
- **Plan:** Day 18, council vote on Sallow's loan; passes 2-1 (Ostry, Ferrant: Ferrant
  is already in the House's debt and frightened) unless the box is clearly heading for
  4,000, or Ferrant is given a reason, or Ostry is exposed. Day 20, the head-tax:
  if the strike holds they send the watch to collect, which turns ugly.

### The Commons · seed omen: Commoner, enemy

- **Wenna Tasker**, 30, tanner, stained to the elbow, funny, angry. Her brother Pell died
  in the Old Tally, which the Ferrants, Ostrys and Dunmores owned. She and Maud were
  friends as girls. **Want:** a seat at the charter. If the commons pay, the commons sign
  and get a say. **Won't:** let children be hurt, of any family. That is where her
  movement will split from her.
- **Garrow Pike**, 50s, a carter, the hard edge of the Commons: let the founders burn,
  and if the watch comes for the tax, the watch can burn too.
- **The Commons as an enemy:** not monsters, a crowd. Commoners (CR 0, club +2) and a few
  Toughs and Bandits among the carters. In a fight they are a mob, and they are people:
  every one hurt has a name the town knows.
- **Means:** numbers (most of the town), the strike (about 1,100 GP of ground-rent
  withheld), Sallow's free legal advice (true but half: the commons aren't liable in
  blood, but on default the dragon takes all goods within the walls, theirs included).
  Wenna knows the second half and is playing chicken; Garrow doesn't care, he has
  nothing.
- **Plan:** the strike holds past Day 20. If the loan passes on Day 18 (secured on the
  ground of Lessing, which means the commons' homes), Garrow's people break into the Moot
  Hall on Day 19 to burn the **land rolls**: no rolls, no record of who owns the ground,
  nothing to pledge. Wenna tries to stop it, since it ends in the watch's clubs, and
  fails unless helped.
- **What would turn them:** a real path to a seat at the charter (the amendment clause),
  or the founders visibly paying first (Ostry's hidden 500, Aldous's house).

### The House of Accounts · seed omen: Erinyes, enemy

- **Corvin Sallow**, 40s, Witness and notary, human. Grey coat, ink on the right
  forefinger, very good manners. Open about what he is; the town uses him for wills
  because he never cheats. **He cannot lie in writing.** He can lie aloud and mostly
  doesn't bother. His own soul belongs to the House (a debt of his father's, inherited);
  he is promised it back after one great acquisition. A default (20 souls of founders'
  blood) would do it; so would the House owning a whole town's ground.
- **Nib**, his imp clerk (Imp, CR 1), lives as a spider in the ledger cabinet. Spiteful,
  will sell small secrets for sugar and for insults to Sallow.
- **The ledger** in that cabinet: every loan in Lessing, Aldous's included. A theft
  target, guarded by Nib, an *Arcane Lock*-style lock (DC 20 to pick), and the fact that
  Sallow would know by morning.
- **Want:** a default or, better, a loan.
- **Won't:** hinder the payment (the charter would give him to the Erinyes himself). He
  can make offers, give true advice, lend, and buy.
- **The loan:** 2,300 GP at once, to the council, 2,600 to repay at next year's Feast,
  "secured upon the ground of Lessing". The town is structurally short, so it won't
  repay, and next year the House owns every plot: the commons' landlord becomes Dis. No
  souls in it at all; Sallow points that out with some pride. Ostry's sum: "We lose
  the ground, not our children." He'll lend to individuals too, small, on their doors.
- **Plan:** Day 3 he puts the loan to the council in writing. He drafted the strike
  notice for Wenna, free (the clean legal hand on it is his). He'll give the strikers
  food money, interest-free, because the strike serves him. He's at the payment on
  Day 30 either way, and he collects Aldous's house that night.
- **The Erinyes:** arrives only at breach, through the Witness's door, at midnight.
  Not a fight. Through omens she may be felt earlier (dreams of the rope, a burn on the
  charter's counterpart).

## The party

### Maud Ferrant (the companion)

- Aldous's granddaughter, 24. Back two months from the college in the south, where she
  studied evocation; came home because of the rent. Her name is on the charter by
  blood.
- **Want:** the rent paid without the loan. Deeper: her life not owed to anyone on a
  signature she never made. She has a copy of the charter and reads it at night looking
  for a door.
- **Won't:** take the House's money, even to save herself. Strike at a commoner who
  hasn't struck first.
- **Manner:** counts everything (squares, slots, coins), short sentences, dry. Some
  college words she's embarrassed by. An owl familiar, **Clerk**. Fond of her
  grandfather, furious with him for being afraid.
- **Wenna** was her best friend at twelve. They haven't spoken since Pell died.
- **On Tick:** Maud hired her. She needs someone who can open the Old Tally strongroom,
  and she doesn't trust the council's hired swords. She doesn't yet know how Tick feels
  about rent.

### Tick Marrow (the PC, the player's)

- A thief who left home to stop paying rent, in a town that exists by paying rent. Her
  want, a door that's hers, owing nobody, is exactly what the commons want and what the
  charter forbids. She'll understand Wenna at once; she's travelling with Maud.
- Hooks: she wants to know how much is in the box and who counts it (Ibbet; Sallow
  witnesses); she reads Draconic, so she can read the Landlady's letters, which in this
  town is power. Her fence downriver talked, so someone from downriver may come looking
  for her: a good use for a future NPC omen.
- Thief-shaped options: Ostry's hidden 500; Sallow's ledger; the toll-house box; the
  rent box itself; the land rolls (the same thing Garrow wants to burn).
- Don't steer her. Let the world make offers: Sallow will offer her a loan for a house.
  A house with her name on the deed, Lessing land, which means a lease from the
  Landlady. The question of whether you can ever really own a door here is the whole
  campaign in small.

## Chapter 1 opening (Day 1)

- Morning, the Lantern. Maud has hired Tick (agreed fee: 20 GP and a share of the
  bounty). The bounty notice on the board. The party is rested.
- What's happening: at dawn the strike notice was nailed to the Rent Office door, in a
  clean legal hand. A crowd reads it. Wenna is there, not speechmaking, answering
  questions; Garrow loud behind her. Maud knows Sallow's hand at once.
- Sallow is at the Lantern having breakfast, polite to everyone.
- Ibbet Crane at the Rent Office counts the box every morning at the window, openly.
  Anyone can watch.
- The omen at chapter start sets the scene's extra colour; then a second omen when the
  party sets out for the mine (or wherever they go).

## What I don't know yet (decide in play, by omen)

- What's in the Old Tally's deep level.
- Whether the Landlady can be petitioned, and how one would get to her alive.
- Who from downriver comes for Tick.

## Second reading (Day 0), what changed

Fixed: households (130, not 500); the gap made structural; the loan on the ground, not
souls by lot; the Landlady not indifferent; rent in kind at resale value; thief-shaped
targets; why the ingots weren't fetched; the land rolls instead of loan papers; Nib a
spider; no tavern speech. Still to watch: the obvious reads (the loan is a trap; Maud and
Wenna reconcile; Sallow offers Tick a house). Let them happen only if play earns them,
and look for the second reading of each when it comes up.
