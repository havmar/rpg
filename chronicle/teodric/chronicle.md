# The chronicle of Teodric Ashmantle

## 1. The missing stone

I came down the Greyway to Tollen on the third day of autumn, with my *Register* under my
arm and eleven waystones copied since I left the hold. Tollen's stone was to be the twelfth.
It wasn't there. Where it had stood for four hundred years by the ford there was a hole, wet
earth, and a furrow running off the road towards the Fenwood.

The reeve, a tired woman named Hesk, said it went two nights ago. No one heard a thing. The
sheep in the near field were found the next morning, three of them, opened up and left. The
village thinks it's thieves after the old iron in the stone's base. Nobody has gone to look.

I went to look. The furrow told me plenty (Survival 25): the stone was dragged, not carried,
by three walkers on two legs, barefoot, with long toes and heavier than any man. They didn't
stop to rest. Whatever they are, they went straight into the fen and they knew the way.

Back at the hole I opened the *Register* to Tollen. My great-grandmother copied this stone
(History 23), and I know the line by heart before I find it: *Here the road keeps what the
marsh gave back.* All the Greyway stones carry a line like that, and I always took them for
poetry. Standing over the empty hole with the reeve's dead sheep downwind, I don't any more.
The stones weren't marking the road. They were holding something down.

I told Hesk I'd follow the furrow in the morning. She offered me a bed and a meal and no
help at all, which is about what I expected.

- Teodric: HP 13/13, all resources full. 15 GP.

## 2. What the marsh gave back

### Into the Fenwood

I left Tollen at first light. The furrow was easy to follow over the fields, and harder once
the alders closed in and the ground turned to black water and grass tussocks. The draggers
hadn't cared where they put their feet. I had to.

Not carefully enough. Around midday I stepped onto what looked like moss over firm peat
(Perception 12) and went in to the thigh, then to the waist. Fen mud doesn't let go. Twice I
heaved and only sank further (Strength saves 7 and 7); my quiver tipped and six arrows slid
into the muck and were gone. In the end I got my quarterstaff across two tussocks, leaned on
it, and dragged myself out (13, with advantage) like a cork from a bottle. I lay there a
while, soaked and shaking, thinking that the *Register* had stayed dry and that this was the
right order of priorities.

After that I went slower and found the better way (Survival 14): a low ridge of old dressed
stone under the reeds, running straight east into the fen. Dwarf work, or something that
learned from dwarves. The Greyway once went through the Fenwood, not around it. Nobody in my
hold ever wrote that down. The furrow ran along it, so did I, and the day was nearly spent
when the ridge brought me to a black mere with an islet in the middle.

### The islet

The Tollen stone lay on its side at the water's edge, half sunk. Around the islet stood a
ring of rotten posts, carved once, and among them three figures stood waiting in the dusk,
up to their shins in water. They didn't move the way people move. Brown leather skin,
sunken faces, bellies sewn with reed: bog bodies, the old dead of the fen, standing up.

I'm a dwarf; dusk is broad daylight to me, and they didn't see me crouched on the ridge
sixty feet off (Stealth 13). I strung the bow, put the Hunter's Mark on the nearest, and
loosed.

### The fight on the causeway

The first arrow took the nearest corpse in the chest (hit, 9 damage) and it barely
flinched, only turned, slower now, with the arrow dragging at it. All three came for me,
wading, at a walk. They don't hurry. They don't need to.

I shot the marked one again and it should have fallen (10 damage), and didn't; it just kept
walking with half its ribs gone (Undead Fortitude, 1 HP left). I backed along the causeway
and my next arrow went wide. The one after that took it through the eye (a critical) and it
went down in the reeds and stayed down. I moved the mark to the second.

Then I ran out of causeway: behind me was the stretch where I'd nearly drowned. So I stood.
I put one more arrow into the second (10 damage) and then the third was on me, and its fist
came down on my shoulder like a mallet (4 damage). I dropped the bow and drew my shortsword.

What followed was ugly and short. I cut the third across the belly (8 damage) and it hardly
noticed. The second reached me and I put the sword through it (7 damage) and it folded
into the mud. The third hit me again, in the ribs, hard (7 damage), and I tasted blood and
knew one more like that would finish me (2 HP). I went for it anyway; it was that or run
into the bog. The first cut should have ended it (11 damage) and it stood up again with its
guts out (Undead Fortitude, natural 17). Its fist went past my ear. The second cut took its
head half off, and it fell and didn't get up.

I sat down on the causeway in the dark and said the words for Cure Wounds with my hand on my
side (17 healing, back to full). I found two of my arrows whole. Eleven left.

### The stone, and the boots

I searched the islet properly (Perception 21), and I found the two things that matter.

The first was the Tollen stone itself. Its inscription has been chiselled off, fresh, the
cuts still pale. Someone didn't want the stone broken; they wanted the words gone, and
then the stone sunk where no one would set it back.

The second was a print in the mud by the posts: a boot, hobnailed, smaller than a man's.
Whoever it was stood here and watched the dead work, then walked east along the causeway
with a light (I found the snuffed candle stub). And caught on a post, as if dropped in a
hurry, a rag of linen with a charcoal rubbing on it. I know that lettering. It's the
Wending stone, two days east on the Greyway: *Here the road remembers the names the water
took.*

They took a rubbing first. That's what I'd do. That's what I've been doing for a month.
Someone is working down the Greyway with the same list I carry, one stone ahead of me.

I didn't understand the carvings on the posts (Arcana 10), only that they were old and
meant something, and that the place felt better with the dead lying down again. I slept on
the islet with my back to a post and my sword across my knees, and nothing came.

At dawn I went back to Tollen, told Hesk her stone was sunk in the fen and her sheep-killers
would kill no more, and that she should set a watch anyway. Then I took the road east to
Wending.

- Teodric: long rest on the islet. HP 13/13, all resources full. 11 arrows. 15 GP.
