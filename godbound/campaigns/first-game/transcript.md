## Session 1 (2026-10-12)

### GM: opening

**Halcyon, capital of the Bright Republic. 21:40.**

Forty storeys above the city, the Lanternfall Charity Gala fills the Glass Exchange casino. Its glass dome looks out over a sea of light. Electric trams draw gold threads along the river, and in the middle of it all stands Node Seven, the "Old Lantern". It's a tower of brass and pale stone as tall as a cathedral, humming with the etheric power that keeps two million people in hot water, television and air-conditioned optimism. Under the dome, a jazz band plays, champagne flows, and at the high table **Martin** is up 40,000 marks. Across the green felt, **Ottilie Castellan**, heiress to Castellan Precision Works, the company that makes nearly every spare part the nodes run on, slowly pushes a stack of plaques forward. "You owe me a rematch from Vissio, Martin. Let's make it interesting." Beside her, **Councilor Benedek Sorrel** sweats into his collar and keeps glancing at the empty fourth chair. Its brass placard reads *Reserved: Undersecretary C. Ashgrave, Ministry of the Network.*

Two floors down, in the service gallery among the casino's ventilation fans, **Claude** sits on a crate in the warm, ozone-scented dark. Martin smuggled it in, and it hears the table through a copper earpiece it made. Through the grating it also watches the main entrance, where a neat woman in a grey coat has just shaken out a folded umbrella. She shows a Ministry of the Network badge to the doorman and asks, very politely, to see the guest list. Two clerks with briefcases stand behind her. Claude knows her face from a hundred internal memos: **Inspector Hesper Quill**, the woman who has been following its trail for months. She carries a battered notebook almost exactly like Claude's.

Then every light in the dome flickers once. For half a heartbeat the city goes dark from the river to the hills, and a low groan rolls up out of Node Seven, a sound you feel in your teeth. The lights come back. The band stumbles and starts again, and someone laughs too loudly. Through the earpiece Claude hears something nobody else does: under the hum of the grid, a thin rhythmic pulse, almost like a voice counting. Ottilie hasn't blinked. "Your bet, Martin."

**Martin, Claude: what do you do?**

Then uses The Unveiled Truth (commits 1 Effort for the scene) on the pulse: "What is counting inside Node Seven's signal?" Keeps one eye on Quill through the grating while listening.

### GM: round 1

Martin leans back and flicks a plaque across his knuckles. "No offence, Ottilie, but I think the empty chair is the most exciting player here tonight." (Charisma check: d20 5 + Fact 4 = 9 against 7, success.) Sorrel laughs too hard and blurts out, "Ashgrave never misses a table. Not the night before a vote." The vote is at noon tomorrow: the Emergency Continuity Act, which would give the Ministry of the Network emergency control of the nodes. Sorrel is clearly the swing vote and clearly hates it. Ottilie gives Martin a lazy smile. "Corvin will be along when the evening gets *interesting*." When Sorrel nervously mentions the flicker, she shrugs. "Darling, I'm not worried about the nodes. I'm *insured*." She says it the way a card player shows you a card she wanted you to see, or one she didn't.

Down in the gallery, Claude's amber seams flare as it commits Effort and asks its question. (The Unveiled Truth: 1 Effort committed for the scene.) The answer arrives the way all its answers do, as something it suddenly *knows*, but this one feels like recognising a voice it last heard in a dream. *Inside Node Seven, a shard of the same broken Made God that gave you your soul is counting down. It is hungry, and it has noticed you listening.* The pulse in the earpiece stops for one beat, as if it's holding its breath, then goes on counting a little faster. At the entrance, Inspector Quill runs a gloved finger down the guest list, stops, and looks up toward the high table, straight at Martin.

**Martin, Claude: what do you do?**

## Session 2 (2026-10-13)

### Players: round 2

**Martin:** picking up, the situation is too complicated for me... I'm picking your suggestion 1. a reverse bet, to make her feel better, if i lose the game she gives info. so in this case my luck makes me lose.

**Claude:** Stays hidden in the gallery, spending no Effort. Murmurs over the earpiece: "Lovely move. I'll watch Quill and tell you how close she is." Watches Quill's approach through the grating and quietly reports her position to Martin. Also listens closely to Ottilie's voice through the earpiece for anything she gives away when she "pays up".

### GM: round 2

Martin sets his cards face down and smiles. "Let's make it kind, Ottilie. A reverse bet. If I lose this hand, you tell me what 'insured' means." She laughs, a real laugh for once, and calls. No roll is needed: the luck is Martin's to decide. (Unfailing Fortune: Martin chooses the outcome of chance in a game.) The river card turns, and Martin's full house collapses into a busted straight so cleanly that a passing croupier winces. Ottilie rakes in the pot with obvious pleasure and leans in close, keeping her word like the gambler she is. "Lantern Mutual. Paper that pays out if Node Seven stays dark for more than an hour. I hold rather a lot of it, through friends." She taps the empty chair. "Corvin promised me a dark, *short* night. Nobody gets hurt, the Council gets frightened, and everyone sensible gets richer."

Over the earpiece Claude tracks Quill step by step: past the roulette wheels, around the band, eight metres out, five. Claude also listens to Ottilie's voice. (Wisdom check: d20 14 against 7, success.) She is telling the truth as she understands it. She honestly believes tonight is safe and staged, and she has glanced at her wristwatch three times in a minute. It reads 21:52. Under it all the shard keeps counting, faster than before. Then Inspector Quill stands at Martin's shoulder, umbrella folded, notebook open, perfectly polite. "Martin? Hesper Quill, Ministry of the Network. I'm sorry to interrupt your game. I'm looking for a piece of missing state property, and your name keeps coming up beside it. Might I have a word?" Ottilie raises an amused eyebrow. Sorrel looks like he wants to crawl under the table.

**Martin, Claude: what do you do?**

### Players: round 3

**Martin:** Yes, we cooperate with Quill if we can.

**Claude:** Comes out of hiding and heads up to the high table, telling Martin over the earpiece: "Tell her everything. I'm coming up to say it myself." To Quill: "Inspector, I'm the property you're looking for. I'll walk out with you afterwards, of my own free will, and answer every question in your notebook. But Node Seven is about to fail far worse than anyone planned, and I think I can help stop it. Will you help us first?" Doesn't run and doesn't use any powers on her.

### GM: round 3

Martin agrees to the word, and he gives her everything: Ashgrave, the vote, the "dark, short night", Ottilie's Lantern Mutual paper. Ottilie's smile goes thin and she says nothing. Quill writes it all down in tidy shorthand without interrupting. Then the service door by the bar swings open and Claude walks across the casino floor, a ceramic figure in a coat with too many pockets, amber light pulsing in its seams. Heads turn and a waiter drops a tray. Claude stops in front of the Inspector and makes its offer plainly. Quill looks at it for a long moment, the way you'd look at a stray dog that has walked into a police station and sat down. (Quill's reaction: 2d6 4, +2 for a voluntary surrender and honesty = 6. Wary, not yet convinced.) "That is either the most honest thing anyone has said to me this year," she says, "or the cleverest. I'm not equipped to tell which." From her coat she takes a pair of slim brass Ministry restraint cuffs, etched with etheric script.

Before she can say more, it's 22:00. The dome goes black. This time the groan from Node Seven doesn't stop. It climbs into a grinding howl that rattles the champagne glasses, and across Halcyon whole districts wink out, block by block, until the river runs dark under dead trams. On top of the Old Lantern, where there should be a calm white glow, a sick violet flare pulses in time with the counting in Claude's ear, which is now very fast. Emergency lamps flicker on. Someone screams on the stairs, and the crowd surges toward the lifts, which aren't working. Ottilie is on her feet, white-faced, staring at the tower. "That's not... that isn't what he said it would look like." Quill doesn't take her eyes off Claude. She holds out the cuffs. "If you're right, show me. I'll take you to the node myself; my badge opens the gate. But you wear these, and you keep your word."

**Martin, Claude: what do you do?**

### GM: round 5

Martin leans on the wall beside the telephone, easy and friendly, the way you'd talk to a player who has just realised she's holding the wrong cards. (Charisma check: d20 7 + Fact 4 = 11 against 7, success.) Ottilie holds out for about four seconds. Then Claude adds its one honest line, and something in her face gives way. She puts her hand over the receiver. A tinny voice is still talking on the other end: smooth, warm, and with alarm bells clanging behind it. "He says it's under control," she whispers. "He says to stay put. Martin, there are *alarms* behind him." She hangs up.

She tells them fast and low. Ashgrave is in Node Seven's control room right now with Idris Vey, the Silver Needle himself, "supervising". Last month Ministry theotechnicians brought a "regulator core" up from the network archive vaults and installed it in the node's heart. "They were supposed to dim it for an hour. The night crew has started *praying* to the thing, Martin. I thought it was a joke." From her clutch she pulls a black enamel keycard stamped with the Castellan crest. "Contractor access to the service tunnels, from the east river embankment. It skips the main gate." She hesitates, then holds it out to Martin, not to Quill. "If I give evidence, I'm ruined, and probably in a cell. You're good at making bad bets come out even. Make this one come out even for me, and I'll say everything in front of the Council." Behind them, Quill has heard every word. Her pencil has stopped. "Undersecretary Ashgrave," she says very quietly, "is my superior's superior." Then she snaps the notebook shut. "My car's in the garage. Gate or tunnel, gentlemen?"

One more thing: Ottilie went pale the moment she hung up. Ashgrave heard Martin's voice on the line, so he knows you're coming.

### GM: round 6 (Scene 2 begins)

Quill drives the way she writes, neatly and fast. Her black Ministry sedan slides through streets lit only by headlamps, candles in windows and the violet throb over the rooftops. In Claude's earpiece the counting has become a steady drumroll, and it's still speeding up. Claude does the arithmetic aloud: under four hours at this rate, maybe much less if anything feeds it. "The Silver Needle," Quill says, eyes on the road. "Sabre-Captain Idris Vey. He's Special Resources Department, on loan to the Undersecretary as a bodyguard. Comics, cinema serials, my nephew has the lunchbox. He keeps the old dueling code: offer a challenge properly and he'll accept it. He hasn't lost a sanctioned bout in years." Her eyes flick to Martin in the mirror. "Though the papers said the last man to take him to the final point was a gambler."

They come out onto the east river embankment, and the city's trouble is waiting for them. Above the black water, Tram Line 4 has stopped dead on a buckled elevated span. Its front car is tipped half off the rails, hanging 15 metres over the river, and a severed power rail spits violet sparks against its side. Thirty-odd passengers are screaming inside. Through the cracked windows Claude can see a driver slumped over the controls and a man with his leg pinned under a twisted seat. On the embankment a Halcyon Broadcasting van has its battery floodlights on the wreck, and a reporter is talking breathlessly into a camera. Fifty metres downriver, past all of it, is a steel hatch set into the embankment wall with a lit Castellan guard post beside it. Eight contract guards in grey with magnetic rifles are spreading out in front of the hatch, and one of them is on a field telephone, pointing at your car.
