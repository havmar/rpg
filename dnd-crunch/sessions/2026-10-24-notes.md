# Session notes: 24 October 2026

The first D&D Crunch session: what the game ended up being, how it got there, and what was
interesting along the way. The full session is in
[2026-10-24-transcript.md](2026-10-24-transcript.md).

## What the game ended up being

**D&D Crunch** is D&D 2024 (the SRD 5.2.1 in `reference/srd/`) played as a tactics log
instead of a story. The rules are in [`../CLAUDE.md`](../CLAUDE.md).

- **One PC, Morten**, a human Evoker wizard (he/him), pre-built at level 3 and now level 4.
- **Minimal narration, open table.** Every roll shows its dice, modifiers and target number.
  Monster stats are revealed as soon as Morten sees them. Rooms get one line of facts.
- **Phone-friendly replies.** Plain Markdown, no code blocks or ASCII maps. Positions are
  grid coordinates (A1 is the north-west corner, 1 square = 1.5 m), and everything is metric.
- **House rule 1, Shadowdark-style roll to cast.** No spell slots. Every cast is d20 + INT +
  proficiency vs DC 10 + spell level. A failure loses that spell until a Long Rest, a
  natural 1 also rolls on a d12 mishap table, and a natural 20 doubles one number. For
  spell attacks one roll is both the casting check and the attack.
- **House rule 2, roguelike death.** When Morten dies he revives at camp with full HP and
  all spells, and loses only the coins and gems he was carrying. You wanted a Dark Souls
  feel, so death costs no XP or max HP.
- **The GM plays Morten too.** That's fastest; you can step in at any time.
- **Two campaigns:**
  - *The Sunken Vault* (`campaigns/sunken-vault/`): a hand-made 8-room dungeon. Paused
    after the gatehouse and the library.
  - *The Demo Dungeon* (`campaigns/demo-dungeon/`): 20 floors built by
    `tools/gen_dungeon.py`. Floor N is for a level N character and holds exactly enough XP
    to level up. Each room has 1–4 copies of one random SRD monster, so you see what that
    monster can do. Each floor starts at a camp that works like a Dark Souls bonfire (a
    safe rest, a trader, the respawn point). Morten cleared floor 3 up to the last room.

## How it evolved during the session

1. Folder, rules and dice roller first, then the house rules and a pre-built Morten.
2. Replies switched from code blocks to plain text, because code blocks don't wrap on a phone.
3. Who plays Morten went through three setups (see below) and ended with the GM.
4. You asked for the spectacle of cheese, so Morten started playing to exploit every rule.
5. The hand-made dungeon gave way to the generated 20-floor demo dungeon.
6. Floor 3 got played through to level 4, with a report after every fight.

## Interesting points

**Who should play the PC.** We tried all three options:

- **An AI player subagent** kept Morten honest, since it never saw the GM's secrets. It was
  slow, though: it paused mid-round for every reaction and reroll, until it wrote standing
  orders.
- **A fast Haiku agent** playing a whole fight was not faster. Four rounds took about 4.5
  minutes, and it ended the fight when Morten ran away even though the rats could chase
  him. That led to the rule "running away doesn't end a fight".
- **The GM playing Morten** was the quickest. The cost is trust: nothing enforces that the GM
  ignores what it knows.

**Roll to cast is brutal on a solo low-level wizard.** At +5, every cast has a 20–30% chance
to fizzle and lose the spell for the day.

- In the first boar fight, five spells fizzled one after another, and Morten won on 1 HP.
- Level 4's INT 19 (+6) noticeably helps.
- Two rulings came out of play. First, the casting check counts as an ability check, so
  Poisoned gives Disadvantage on it; that is what killed Morten twice at the Death Dogs.
- Second, Advantage on a spell attack (a target that is Restrained or Unconscious) also
  helps the casting check, since it is the same roll. This made Web plus Scorching Ray the
  best combo of the session.

**Mishaps were memorable.** A tongue-tied wizard got crit by a badger. Unravel ended Mage
Armor mid-fight. A slipped grip dropped staff and lamp while two dogs were biting. A
snuffed lamp went out while Morten hung from a chain in the dark.

**The best cheese, roughly in order:**

1. **Be where they can't reach.** Morten climbed a hanging chain 4.5 m up and lured the two
   Death Dogs in. Those dogs had killed him twice; this time he took no damage.
2. **Sleep, then a point-blank crit.** Attacks on an Unconscious target from within 1.5 m
   always crit. That gave a 19-damage Fire Bolt on a rat swarm and a three-crit Scorching
   Ray on a badger pile.
3. **Web over a chokepoint, then attacks with Advantage.**
4. **Mage Hand heist.** He emptied a guarded lectern without ever going near it, then poured
   oil on the inert skeletons. One Scorching Ray destroyed both before they moved.
5. **Shatter on constructs**, since they roll its save with Disadvantage. Three flying swords
   fell in two rounds.
6. **Sleep and walk past**, because bypassed monsters still give XP. That was the octopus
   (eventually) and a sleeping Darkmantle.
7. **Bribery.** Coins carried are lost on death anyway, so 15 GP bought a satyr's goodwill.
8. **The purse drop.** Before a risky fight he drops his purse on the floor. Coins that
   aren't carried survive a death. This loophole in the respawn rule is still open.
9. **Rest anywhere.** The demo dungeon has no wandering monsters, so any cleared room is as
   safe as camp.

**High difficulty is the real test for a party of one.** Low and Moderate fights went fine with
cheese. Two Death Dogs (4 bites a round, plus poison) killed him twice before the chain
trick.

**The owl familiar died three times**, each time in round 1. It's 1 HP, and a GM habit of
parking it near the action killed it. Keep it out of fights.

**Hot dice.** The session rolled about 430 d20s with 35 natural 20s, about 8% where 5% is
expected. Three Shields came up natural 20, which doubled to +10 AC. The flip side was a
string of natural 1s on concentration and casting checks.

**Encounter math.** Under the 2024 rules, the XP needed per level grows faster than the
encounter budgets in the mid levels. One level-up takes 4–15 encounters (floor 4 has 15
rooms). You chose to keep it by the book.

**GM lesson.** Rolling dice for several steps at once sometimes meant rolls had to be thrown
away, when an earlier result changed the next decision. Now in the rules: roll step by step
whenever a result decides what happens next.

## Where things stand

- **Morten:** Wizard 4, 26 HP, INT 19, XP 2,700. He's carrying 116 GP 5 SP and has no
  rations. Deaths: 3 (1 in the Sunken Vault, 2 at the demo dungeon's 3.8 Death Dogs).
- **Demo Dungeon:** floor 3, everything but room 3.9 (two Copper Dragon Wyrmlings) is done.
  The octopus in 3.2 is still alive and bypassed. The satyr in 3.6 is friendly.
- **Sunken Vault:** paused, with the gatehouse and library cleared.

## Ideas for next time

- Decide whether the purse drop is a feature or a bug.
- Rations matter during long rest-and-retry loops. Morten ran out on floor 3.
- Consider Invisibility (new at level 4) as the big bypass tool.
- If floors 4–8 feel long at 10–15 rooms, the generator could hold the room count steady
  while keeping the XP the same.
